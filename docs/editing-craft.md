# Editing Craft — Stacked Reels (A-roll + B-roll)

> The agents' "editing skill." Distilled from professional talking-head and
> short-form editing practice (sources at bottom). Pair with `cropping-rules.md`.

## Framing a talking person

- **Medium-close shot**: frame from mid-chest/shoulders up. Head AND shoulders
  visible — never a floating head, never just eyes-to-nose.
- **Eyes on the upper third.** Place the speaker's eyes ~1/3 from the top of the
  frame (rule of thirds). This reads as balanced and professional.
- **Headroom: small but present.** A little space above the head. Too much = the
  subject sinks; too little (or clipped) = amateur. For tight social crops keep
  it modest, but NEVER clip the top of the head.
- **No gratuitous zoom.** Don't punch in by default. A natural medium shot beats
  an over-zoomed face. Zoom only when explicitly requested.

## Aspect ratios

- Target canvas for reels: **9:16 vertical** (1080×1920).
- The talking person is always cropped to a **1:1 square** first (see
  `cropping-rules.md`), then placed.
- A reference may also be **16:9 horizontal**; same square-crop principle applies.

## Stacking layouts (2 sources: A-roll + B-roll)

- **Top/bottom split** is the standard for 9:16 when combining a square talking
  head with supporting footage: square on top, B-roll on the bottom (or vice
  versa). Each panel gets clear, uncrowded space.
- **Circle PIP over full-bleed B-roll**: the talking head as a circular overlay
  while B-roll fills the screen — good when the B-roll is the main visual.
- **One side leads.** In any split, one panel is primary and the other supports.
  Don't make both fight for attention.
- Up to ~3–4 stacked sources max before panels get too small on a phone.

## B-roll usage

- B-roll **breaks up** talking-head monotony, adds context, and **covers cuts**
  (jump cuts between A-roll takes are hidden behind B-roll).
- Match B-roll to what's being said when possible (content relevance).
- On talking-head-heavy content, introduce a visual shift every ~10–15s to hold
  attention.

## Pacing & cuts (guardrails)

- **Never cut mid-sentence or mid-word.** Cut at sentence boundaries / natural
  pauses (this is enforced by the trim-validator + silence map).
- **No dead air.** Don't leave trailing silence at a segment's end; don't leave a
  gap before the next begins.
- Keep shots long enough to absorb (~3–5s of meaningful content) but vary the
  rhythm so it doesn't feel static.

## Quality bar (what "professional" means here)

A crop/layout is acceptable only if: head-top not clipped, chin+shoulders
present, eyes near upper third, headroom small-but-present, not over-zoomed,
person ~90% of the square, cuts land on sentence boundaries, no gaps/dead air,
no blank/empty frames at transitions.
